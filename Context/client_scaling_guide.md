# Client Acquisition & Scaling Guide
## Vertical Knowledge Infrastructure for Equipment & Mechanical Service Companies

Geographic Focus: Mid-Atlantic (VA, MD, DC, PA, NJ, NC optional expansion)
Vertical Focus: Equipment & Mechanical Service Companies (No Sub-Niche Yet)

---

# Overview

This document outlines:

- Phase 1: Validation Clients (0–3 customers)
- Phase 2: Repeatability Clients (3–10 customers)
- Phase 3: Expansion Clients (10+ customers)
- Client Research Criteria
- Outreach Strategy
- Engagement Structure
- Qualification Checklist
- Weekly Outreach Execution Plan

Core Product:
Internal searchable knowledge system for field technicians.

Core Value:
Reduce troubleshooting time, speed up training, eliminate senior-tech interruptions.

---

# PHASE 1 — VALIDATION CLIENTS (0–3 Customers)

## Objective

- Validate real-world usage
- Confirm ROI narrative
- Refine onboarding workflow
- Gather testimonials
- Improve messaging

These customers are learning partners, not perfect accounts.

---

## Ideal Phase 1 Traits

Target companies that are:

- Owner-led
- Operationally stressed
- Growing
- Documentation-heavy
- Technician-based
- Not overly corporate

Avoid:

- Franchise chains
- National enterprise service orgs
- Companies with internal IT departments
- Bureaucratic decision processes

---

## Size Criteria

- 5–15 field technicians
- 10–40 total employees
- $2M–$10M revenue
- Regional operations

Why:
Small enough to decide quickly.
Large enough to feel knowledge bottlenecks.

---

# PHASE 2 — REPEATABILITY CLIENTS (3–10 Customers)

## Objective

- Standardize onboarding
- Reduce deployment time
- Strengthen pricing confidence
- Improve analytics layer
- Develop repeatable case studies

---

## Phase 2 Traits

- 10–25 field technicians
- Expanding service footprint
- Hiring regularly
- Multiple equipment brands supported
- Website shows growth and credibility

---

# PHASE 3 — EXPANSION CLIENTS (10+ Customers)

## Objective

- Raise pricing tiers
- Add analytics + admin intelligence
- Move toward multi-tenant architecture
- Position as infrastructure

---

## Phase 3 Traits

- 20–50 technicians
- Multi-location presence
- $10M+ revenue
- Aggressive growth or PE-backed
- Clear operational scaling pressure

# ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY

## Positioning

You are not selling AI.
You are diagnosing workflow inefficiency.

---

## Cold Outreach Template

Subject: Quick question about technician training at [Company Name]

Hi [Name],

I work with equipment service companies in the Mid-Atlantic that struggle with technicians spending too much time digging through manuals or interrupting senior staff for troubleshooting.

We recently helped a service company centralize their internal documentation into a searchable system their field techs can use in real time.

Would you be open to a short conversation about how you currently manage training and documentation?

Best,
[Your Name]

---

No AI buzzwords.
No hard pitch.
Simple, problem-oriented.

---

# FIRST CALL STRUCTURE

Goal: Diagnose pain, not pitch product.

Ask:

- How do your techs access manuals today?
- Where is documentation stored?
- How long does it take to ramp new hires?
- How often do senior techs get interrupted?
- What happens if your best technician leaves?
- How do you handle complex troubleshooting?

Let them describe inefficiency.

Then introduce solution:

"We centralize your manuals and internal knowledge into a searchable internal system your techs can use from their phone in the field."

---

# QUALIFICATION CHECKLIST

After call, evaluate:

- Is there clear documentation chaos?
- Are senior techs overloaded?
- Are they growing?
- Do they hire new technicians regularly?
- Is owner engaged and decision-ready?

If yes to 3+ → strong candidate.

If weak signals → nurture.

---

# PILOT STRUCTURE (Phase 1)

Never offer free deployment.

Position as:

Early Deployment Program
Founding Customer Tier
Initial Implementation Rate

Suggested Early Pricing:

Setup: $3,000–$7,000
Monthly: $1,500–$2,500

---

# WEEKLY OUTREACH EXECUTION PLAN

Week 1:
- Build list of 50 companies
- Send 15–20 emails
- Send 15 LinkedIn connection requests

Week 2:
- Follow up with week 1 emails
- Send 15 new emails
- Continue LinkedIn outreach

Week 3:
- Book 3–5 discovery calls
- Refine messaging
- Track objections

Expected Conversion:
1–2 clients per 30–50 qualified outreach attempts.

---

# FOUNDER GUARDRAILS

- Do not expand into CRM AI yet.
- Do not white-label early.
- Do not chase non-mechanical SMBs.
- Focus on documentation + technician efficiency.
- Keep scope tight.
